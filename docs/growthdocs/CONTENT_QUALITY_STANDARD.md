# Content Quality Standard

## The Standard

Every piece of content published under the Money OS brand must pass a single test:

> **Would a freelancer, agency owner, or founder bookmark this and come back to it?**

If the answer is no — if the content is a keyword-stuffed summary of what Google already shows on page 1 — do not publish it.

This is not a marketing document. It is a commitment that governs every blog post, doc page, and resource we publish.

---

## The Eight Quality Rules

### Rule 1 — Specificity Over Generality

Generic: "There are many ways to track expenses."
Specific: "Here are the 5 expense categories that freelancers most commonly miss at tax time."

Every assertion must be specific. If you cannot make it specific, you do not know enough about the topic to write about it yet.

### Rule 2 — Real Examples Over Hypotheticals

Bad: "For example, a freelancer might have expenses like software subscriptions..."
Good: "If you pay for Figma ($15/mo), Notion ($8/mo), and Loom ($15/mo), that is $456/year in deductible software expenses."

Hypotheticals are filler. Real examples are why people share content.

### Rule 3 — No Obvious Statements

Delete every sentence that the reader already knows before clicking:

- "Expenses are costs incurred during business operations." → Delete
- "Tracking expenses is important for tax purposes." → Delete
- "As a freelancer, you likely use multiple tools." → Delete

If a 10-year-old could have said it, it doesn't belong in a professional resource.

### Rule 4 — Answer Before Qualifying

Bad: "While there are many factors to consider when choosing an expense tracker, and every business has different needs, it ultimately depends on your specific situation..."

Good: "For most freelancers, Money OS or Wave is the right choice. Use Money OS if you want team access and budgets. Use Wave if you need invoicing built in."

Answer the question in the first two sentences. Then qualify, expand, and nuance.

### Rule 5 — Concrete Numbers Over Vague Ranges

Bad: "Expense trackers can save freelancers significant time."
Good: "Switching from manual expense logging in Excel to CSV import in Money OS saves approximately 2-3 hours per month for the average freelancer."

If you don't have the number, say "estimated" or "approximately." But provide a number.

### Rule 6 — No Padding Between Sections

Padding is the empty sentence you write to fill the white space between two real ideas. Common forms:

- "Now that we've covered X, let's move on to Y."
- "This is an important topic that many people struggle with."
- "In conclusion, we've seen that..."

Delete all of these. The H2 heading transitions the reader — no sentence needed.

### Rule 7 — Proof Over Claims

Bad: "Money OS is the best expense tracker for freelancers."
Good: "Money OS has CSV import from any bank, 8 pre-configured freelancer expense categories, and generates a tax-ready summary in one click. Here is what that looks like in practice:"

Claims require proof. Proof can be: a screenshot, a specific feature description, a user quote, a number, or a side-by-side comparison. Unproven claims read as marketing copy and are skipped.

### Rule 8 — Cut Until It Hurts

The first draft of any article is too long. The default editing pass is to remove 20% of the word count without losing meaning. This forces specificity and removes padding simultaneously.

After writing, read each paragraph and ask: "If I deleted this paragraph, would the reader miss something important?" If no, delete it.

## The Quality Checklist

Before any content is published, run through this list:

### Structure

- [ ] Title contains the primary keyword naturally (not stuffed)
- [ ] H1 is benefit-first, not feature-first
- [ ] First paragraph answers the core question or states the value clearly
- [ ] Every H2 section delivers on its heading promise
- [ ] No section is there to pad length — every section adds information

### Writing Quality

- [ ] No generic opener ("In today's digital world..." → Delete)
- [ ] No passive filler ("It is important to note that..." → Delete)
- [ ] Every claim is supported by a specific example, number, or screenshot
- [ ] No obvious statements the target reader already knows
- [ ] No padding transitions between sections
- [ ] Conclusion is optional — if you said it all in the body, skip the conclusion

### Technical SEO

- [ ] Title tag: primary keyword present, under 60 characters
- [ ] Meta description: under 155 characters, contains keyword, has CTA
- [ ] First 100 words: primary keyword appears once
- [ ] At least 2 internal links to related docs or use case pages
- [ ] Alt text on all images
- [ ] FAQPage JSON-LD if FAQs are included

### Accuracy

- [ ] All statistics are cited or marked as estimated
- [ ] All product features described are currently live
- [ ] No promises about future features
- [ ] Tax-related claims include a disclaimer: "Consult a tax professional for advice specific to your situation."

## Content That Will Not Be Published

### Keyword-Stuffed Content

Content that exists only to rank for a keyword but provides no genuine informational value:

"Money OS is a great expense tracker for freelancers. Freelancers need expense trackers. The best expense tracker for freelancers is Money OS. Freelancers who use expense trackers..."

This is rejected, regardless of SEO potential.

### AI-Generated Filler

Content that is:
- Grammatically correct but informationally empty
- Balanced to the point of saying nothing ("it depends" without specifics)
- Structured like an AI article (5-paragraph structure with transitions)
- Filled with hedged non-statements

See `ANTI_AI_SLOP_GUIDELINES.md` for the specific detection criteria.

### False Precision

"Using Money OS, freelancers save 73% of their time on expense tracking."

Without a methodology and source, this is fabricated. Do not publish fake statistics. They undermine all other credibility.

### Negative Competitor Content

No content that:
- Mocks competitor products
- Makes unverifiable negative claims about competitors
- Frames comparisons with loaded language ("Unlike the bloated and confusing Expensify...")

Comparison content must be accurate, fair, and focused on differences — not on attacking.

## Voice and Tone Standards

### Voice: Direct Expert

Write as if you are the most knowledgeable person in the room who is also deeply respectful of the reader's intelligence and time.

- Do not explain things they obviously know
- Do explain things that are genuinely non-obvious or commonly misunderstood
- Write in second person ("you") when the content is instructional
- Write in first person ("we") for product updates and company perspective

### Tone: Confident Without Arrogance

- "Here is how to do X" (not "You might want to consider doing X")
- "Money OS is the right choice if..." (not "Money OS might be a good fit for some users")
- "This approach will not work for..." (not "This approach may not always be ideal for every situation")

### Length: As Short As It Takes, No Shorter

No target word count. The right length is the length required to fully answer the question and no more. Some topics require 500 words. Some require 3,000. Length is not a proxy for quality.

Typical ranges by content type:
- Comparison post: 1,200-2,000 words
- How-to guide: 800-1,500 words
- Tax guide: 1,500-2,500 words
- Product update: 300-600 words
- Resource list: 600-1,000 words

## Quality Review Process

For any content over 800 words, a second-pass review is required before publishing:

**Question 1**: Did I learn something I didn't know before?
If no → major revision needed.

**Question 2**: Is there a paragraph I'd skim on the first read?
If yes → cut or rewrite that paragraph.

**Question 3**: Is every statistic or claim I make verifiable or clearly estimated?
If no → add source or change to "approximately."

**Question 4**: Would I share this with a freelancer friend and not feel embarrassed?
If no → it's not ready.

---

## Enforcement

These standards apply to:
- All blog posts
- All documentation pages
- All resource pages
- All use case page copy
- All comparison pages

They do not apply to:
- Internal planning documents (like this one)
- Database schemas and technical architecture docs
- Code comments

Violation of these standards = the content is revised before publishing, not after. There is no exception for content that ranked well in a different era or for content that an AI wrote fast and cheaply.
